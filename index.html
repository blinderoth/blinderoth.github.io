I. Overview

How I work

I value structure, and I stay with a problem until it is solved. I prefer work that ends in a tangible improvement over work that only describes one.

I want to understand how something works in practice before I change it. Years in a warehouse taught me how quickly a small bottleneck becomes a costly one.

I care about incentives. A good measure should change behavior, not just record it.

I hold a BSc in Business Administration from Lund University and spent an exchange semester in Singapore. I started in Tetra Pak's warehouse and moved into analytical roles in the supply network.

Tetra Pak, Supply Network QualityJun to Sep 2026

Automating a quality workflow

Managing a quality inspection required jumping through five SAP transactions and repeatedly re-entering part and supplier numbers for each part number. On my own initiative, while managing the daily operational work, I built a script in Python and SAP GUI that unified the procedure into a single prompt.

I tested it through several iterations before my colleagues started using it daily. It saves them on average 40 minutes of administrative work per day.

A salary is not an hourly cost.

To price labor honestly, I built the rate up from a machinist’s salary and every cost an employer pays on top. The total is 54% higher than the salary.

  1. Monthly salary, metal machinist (Swedish salary statistics)SEK 34 000
  2. Employer contributions, 31.42% of salary and vacation pay (Skatteverket)+ SEK 11 965
  3. Vacation pay, 12% of salary (Semesterlagen)+ SEK 4 080
  4. Occupational pension, 4.5% (Avtalspension SAF-LO)+ SEK 1 530
  5. Special payroll tax on the pension, 24.26% (Skatteverket)+ SEK 371
  6. Insurances, about 1% (Fora price list 2025)+ SEK 363

LundaEkonomerna, the TreasuryJun 2024 to Jun 2025

Opening up a student union's committee

I started as Vice Project Leader in June 2024 and took over as Project Leader in January 2025.

The treasury committee was the only one within LundaEkonomerna which required Swedish knowledge, and this was mainly due to the documents and work instructions which were used in the role only existing in Swedish. Given that the School of Economics has a large presence of international students (as much as 1/4 of the school) and that LundaEkonomerna generally has an English-first rule a lot of international students applied to member positions within the Treasury committee. These people were unfortunately spending time on an application which would never lead anywhere. I felt that this needed to change and as I stepped up into the role of the Project Leader for the committee I began the process of translating the internal documents from Swedish to English. Given the sensitivity of the information that the committee handles this was a job I left to manual labor - ensuring that the translation didn't mislead any future reader.
