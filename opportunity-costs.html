About the numbers

This is an illustration, not financial advice. Real returns vary from year to year and can be negative.

Money is invested at the start of each month and grows monthly. Fees and taxes are left out. In Sweden, for example, the yearly tax on an ISK account would lower the result.

With today's money switched on, every amount is shown in today's purchasing power, and your costs are assumed to rise with inflation. Resale values drop by a fixed share each year.

The 4% rule is a common rule of thumb from studies of 30-year retirements. The 10% figure is a commonly cited long-run average for the S&P 500 before inflation. Preset amounts in EUR and USD are rounded conversions at 10 to 1.
